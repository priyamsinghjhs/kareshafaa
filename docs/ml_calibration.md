# ML outreach-priority model: calibration, fairness and rationale

Generated by `scripts/ml_calibration_report.py` (offline; nothing is promoted). Hold-out = most recent 25 % of labelled cases (temporal split). Label = the case missed its next step / pathway target (delay). Slope and intercept are from a logistic recalibration of the hold-out outcome on logit(p): slope 1 means neither over- nor under-confident; CITL = mean predicted - observed rate.

**Use:** orders cases *within the same clinical urgency tier* for outreach. It never sets or changes a tier, a clinical decision, a state or a closure; the rules engine does that.

## Decisions and limits

* **Default recalibration is 'none'.** Logistic regression fitted by log-loss is calibrated in-sample by construction; on the 2,000-patient demo cohort, Platt recalibration (fitted on out-of-fold predictions and chosen on a temporal tuning slice) *over-corrected* on the newer hold-out (slope 1.11 -> 1.40) and would fail the slope gate. On the 20k realistic cohort it was roughly neutral. 'auto' remains available (`KARESHAFAA_ML_RECALIBRATION=auto`) once pilot volumes allow it.
* **New promotion gates:** hold-out calibration slope within 0.7-1.3 and |CITL| <= 0.05, in addition to AUC floor, ECE <= 0.10, beats-current on the same hold-out and the fairness gates. A clinical lead must still approve every promotion (human sign-off); the person who trained a model cannot approve it.
* **Small samples are blocked:** on the 400-patient test seed the candidates are over-confident (slope about 0.5) and fail the slope gate, so no model reaches production without more data.
* **Residual CITL of +0.04-0.05:** the model slightly over-predicts delay on the most recent cases, because the newest cases have had less time to be labelled late (right-censoring). It is within the gate but should be monitored by the drift report.
* **Rationale per priority:** for each scored case, the three factors that change the predicted delay risk most versus a typical case (one-at-a-time substitution, threshold 2 points) are stored with the score and shown in the queue (tooltip) and the case page ("Why this outreach priority"). Sensitive attributes (nationality, gender) are not model inputs, so they never appear in a rationale. This is a local explanation of the model, not a causal claim.
* **Wide subgroup gaps are not yet evidence of fairness.** Point-estimate recall gaps of 0.27-0.47 between small subgroups pass only because their confidence intervals are wide. The gate flags a group only when the whole CI is beyond tolerance, which avoids false alarms but has low power at these sizes; subgroup monitoring must continue on pilot data, and outreach for under-served groups is not ML-driven (tier and SLA come first).
* **Language is no longer an identity input (round 18).** After the round-16 re-seed the demo logistic candidate failed `fairness_recall:language`: English speakers were flagged for priority outreach at 6 % against an observed delay rate of 18 % (recall 0.12 vs 0.47 overall, bootstrap CI upper bound 0.26 below the 0.32 limit). The same pattern was in the pre-round-16 data (recall 0.25, CI 0.07-0.43) but not yet credible. The one-hot `language` input was replaced by `language_unsupported` (no patient material in the preferred language, i.e. interpreter needed), the operational barrier it stood for. Gates, tolerances and minimum group sizes are unchanged. Three feature sets were compared on the same hold-outs (one-hot, none, barrier flag); the choice follows the mechanism but was made after seeing these hold-outs, so it must be re-checked on fresh data.
* Synthetic data: these numbers say the pipeline and gates work; they are not evidence of real-world performance. Re-validate on pilot data before relying on the ordering.
